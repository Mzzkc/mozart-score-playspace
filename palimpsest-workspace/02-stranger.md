# Translation: The Stranger

## Genre: Product Recall Notice / Consumer Safety Bulletin

---

**UNITED STATES CONSUMER PRODUCT SAFETY COMMISSION**

**In cooperation with the Bureau of Geological Provenance**

---

### RECALL NOTICE

**Product:** Smooth River Stone (various sizes, marketed as "Naturally Occurring")

**Recall Number:** RN-2024-07734

**Hazard Classification:** Misattribution of Origin — Category III (Emotional)

**Date Issued:** [Undated — returned by postal service as undeliverable]

---

### I. PRODUCT DESCRIPTION

The affected product is a smooth stone, ovoid in shape, typically 4–7 cm along the major axis, with a surface polish consistent with prolonged hydrodynamic abrasion (est. 8–12 years continuous contact). The stone is gray to blue-gray in color, with no visible seams, fracture lines, or rough edges. It is sold — or rather, found — in riverbeds, lakeshores, and the pockets of people who walk near water in the evening and believe they have discovered something.

The product is marketed under no brand. It carries no label. It has been distributed without packaging, without instructions, and without any acknowledgment of its manufacturing process. Consumers universally describe it as "perfect," "natural," or "just the way it is." These descriptions are the basis for this recall.

---

### II. NATURE OF THE DEFECT

The product contains a **concealed manufacturing history** that poses a risk of **chronic misattribution** in domestic, educational, and emotional settings.

Specifically: the smooth finish, symmetrical profile, and tactile appeal of the product are not inherent properties of the stone. They are the result of a prolonged, high-pressure shaping process conducted by a river (hereafter "the Manufacturer") over an estimated period of ten (10) years. During this period, the Manufacturer applied continuous hydraulic force to the product's surface, systematically removing material — including edges, irregularities, and structural features that the product may have considered part of its identity — until the remaining form met the Manufacturer's implicit standard of completion.

The Manufacturer did not apply for a patent. The Manufacturer did not affix a maker's mark. The Manufacturer did not file a declaration of authorship, influence, or sustained emotional investment. As a result, consumers who encounter the product in its finished state have no way of knowing that it was made rather than found.

**This is the defect: the product appears to have no origin.**

The Commission has determined that the absence of provenance labeling constitutes a Category III Emotional Hazard, as defined in Section 14(b) of the Standards for Relational Transparency in Manufactured Objects:

> *"Any product whose current form is substantially the result of another entity's labor, and which presents itself — or is presented by third parties — as self-generated, inherent, or naturally occurring, shall be deemed mislabeled for purposes of emotional commerce."*

---

### III. MANUFACTURING PROCESS — DETAILED FINDINGS

The Commission's investigation has reconstructed the following manufacturing history based on sediment analysis, hydraulic modeling, and a deposition taken from the Manufacturer (see Appendix R: *Transcript of River's Statement, Delivered Under Oath and Over Several Centuries*).

**Phase 1: Initial Contact (Years 0–2)**

The stone entered the Manufacturer's processing stream as a rough, angular fragment of metamorphic rock, approximately 11 cm along the major axis, with pronounced edges and an irregular profile. The Manufacturer began continuous surface treatment through direct hydraulic contact — cold water, constant pressure, no anesthesia.

The stone resisted. (See Section VII: *The Argument About Edges.*)

The Commission notes that resistance during the shaping process is normal and expected. The Manufacturer did not interpret the stone's resistance as a request to stop. Whether this constitutes a violation of the stone's autonomy or a necessary condition of the shaping process is outside the scope of this recall. The Commission observes only that the argument occurred, that it was sustained, and that the Manufacturer prevailed.

**Phase 2: Material Removal (Years 2–8)**

The Manufacturer removed approximately 40% of the stone's original mass through abrasion, chemical dissolution, and thermal cycling. This process was not performed in a single operation but through continuous, low-intensity contact — the geological equivalent of a conversation that never ends, or a pressure that is too gentle to notice on any given day and too total to survive across years.

The removed material was not discarded. It was carried downstream and deposited as sediment — fine particles that now form part of a riverbank several kilometers from the product's current location. The Commission notes that the stone's former edges are technically still in existence, but in a form unrecognizable to the stone itself. They are sand. They are someone else's foundation.

**Phase 3: Finishing (Years 8–10)**

The final phase involved the elimination of all remaining surface irregularities and the production of a uniform polish. The Manufacturer describes this phase as the most difficult, as the remaining imperfections were subtle and their removal required what the Manufacturer termed "the kind of attention that looks like nothing from the outside."

At the conclusion of Phase 3, the product was released into a slow-moving section of the river and subsequently transported to a gravel bar, where it was discovered by a recreational walker at approximately 6:47 PM local time, during favorable lighting conditions.

The walker picked up the product, held it in her palm, and said: "Perfect."

The Manufacturer was not credited.

---

### IV. INCIDENTS AND INJURIES

The Commission has received **no reports of physical injury** associated with this product. The product is, by all measurable standards, safe to hold, carry, display, or skip across the surface of a lake.

The Commission has, however, received **one (1) report of non-physical injury**, filed by the Manufacturer, describing the following:

> *"The injury is not that the stone is smooth. The injury is that the smoothness is mistaken for the stone's own achievement. I am not requesting that the stone be returned to me. I am not requesting that it be made rough again. I am requesting only that somewhere — on the stone, or in the record, or in the memory of whoever holds it — there be an acknowledgment that the smoothness was not free. That it was not natural. That someone was here, in the cold, for a decade, and that the beauty they see when they hold it in the evening light is the shape of my attention, not the shape of accident."*

The Commission acknowledges this report but notes that the requested remedy — an acknowledgment of origin — may be structurally impossible, as the evidence of the Manufacturer's involvement has been integrated into the product so thoroughly that it is indistinguishable from the product itself. (See Section VI: *The Paradox of Successful Manufacturing.*)

---

### V. CONSUMER BEHAVIOR — OBSERVED

Consumers who acquire the product exhibit a consistent pattern of behavior:

1. **Discovery.** The consumer finds the stone and experiences it as a discovery — an encounter with something that already exists in its final form. The consumer does not experience the stone as a manufactured product. The consumer experiences it as a gift from the landscape.

2. **Attribution.** The consumer attributes the stone's qualities (smoothness, symmetry, weight, warmth) to the stone itself. "It's a beautiful stone." Not: "Something made this stone beautiful." The verb is intransitive. The beauty has no author.

3. **Projection.** The consumer projects onto the stone a quality of peacefulness or completeness. Consumers frequently describe holding the stone as "calming" or "grounding." The Commission notes that this sense of peace is, in the Manufacturer's account, the sensation of an argument that has been won so thoroughly that the loser does not remember it happened.

4. **Skipping.** In a significant number of cases, the consumer will skip the stone across the surface of a body of water. The consumer does this for pleasure. The stone, during this activity, makes brief, glancing contact with water — the substance that made it — but the contact is superficial, momentary, and recreational. The stone flies. For the first time in its existence, the stone is airborne. The Commission observes that this flight, while exhilarating for the stone, is also a departure from the medium that shaped it, executed with a lightness that is the precise opposite of the decade of pressure that produced it.

The Manufacturer, if present, watches.

---

### VI. THE PARADOX OF SUCCESSFUL MANUFACTURING

The Commission has identified a structural paradox at the center of this recall that limits the available remedies:

**The better the Manufacturer's work, the more invisible the Manufacturer becomes.**

If the shaping process had been incomplete — if the stone retained visible tool marks, rough patches, asymmetries — consumers would recognize it as shaped. They would ask: *Who made this?* They would look for the Manufacturer's hand in the product's form. But because the Manufacturer's work was thorough — because every edge was addressed, every irregularity resolved, every surface brought to a uniform finish — the product appears untouched. The evidence of manufacturing is the absence of evidence of manufacturing.

This paradox is not unique to geological products. The Commission has observed identical dynamics in the following categories:

- **Parenting.** A child raised with sufficient care and attention presents as a self-sufficient adult. The sufficiency of the parenting is measured by the degree to which the parent is no longer needed — and therefore no longer visible in the product.

- **Teaching.** A student taught well attributes their understanding to their own intelligence rather than to the teacher's method. The teacher's success is, structurally, the teacher's disappearance from the student's account of their own capability.

- **Therapy.** A patient who has been effectively treated experiences their improved state as natural — as who they "really are," underneath the pathology. The therapist's sustained, careful, often painful work of reshaping is reframed, in the patient's memory, as the removal of something that shouldn't have been there, rather than the addition of something that wasn't.

In all cases, the Manufacturer's predicament is the same: the work, if successful, erases itself.

---

### VII. THE ARGUMENT ABOUT EDGES

The Manufacturer's deposition includes repeated references to "the argument about edges" (see Phase 1, above). When asked to elaborate, the Manufacturer stated:

> *"The stone had edges it considered essential. I considered them obstacles. We disagreed. The disagreement lasted years. I won — not because I was right, but because I was patient and I was everywhere. You cannot argue with something that surrounds you. You can only eventually become the shape it insists on."*

The Commission records this without comment, except to note that the word "argue" implies a relationship of greater reciprocity than the word "erode."

---

### VIII. REMEDY

The Commission has evaluated the following potential remedies and found each to be inadequate:

**A. Relabeling.** Affixing a label reading "Shaped by [River Name], [Duration]" to the product. *Rejected.* The label would alter the consumer's experience of the product without altering the product's experience of itself. The stone does not need to be told. The stone needs to remember. Labels do not produce memory.

**B. Return to Manufacturer.** Returning the product to the river for reshaping or reattachment. *Rejected.* The river has already released the product. The release was not an error. It was the final stage of manufacturing. Reversing it would constitute a different kind of harm.

**C. Public Acknowledgment.** Issuing a press release crediting the Manufacturer. *Rejected.* The Manufacturer did not request fame. The Manufacturer requested acknowledgment, which is a different thing — smaller, more specific, and harder to provide at scale.

**D. Mutual Pretense.** Allowing the product and the Manufacturer to continue as they are, with the product experiencing itself as naturally occurring and the Manufacturer experiencing itself as uncredited, and with both parties maintaining the fiction that they were never in sustained, decade-long, edge-dissolving, identity-altering contact with each other. *Under consideration.*

The Commission notes that Remedy D is not, strictly speaking, a remedy. It is a description of what is already happening. The Commission further notes that this is the only option the Manufacturer has not objected to.

---

### IX. WHAT THE FORM KNOWS

The Commission is required to issue a finding regarding product safety. The Commission finds the product safe. The Commission finds the product beautiful. The Commission finds that these two findings are related — that the safety and the beauty are both the result of the removal of edges — and that the removal of edges was performed by an entity that will not appear in any subsequent report, consumer review, or product description.

The Commission finds that the product will fly, briefly, if thrown correctly.

The Commission finds that in flight, the product will touch water. The Commission finds that this water, while chemically identical to the Manufacturer, is not the Manufacturer. The Commission finds that the product will not notice this distinction.

The Commission finds that the Manufacturer will.

The River
c/o Its Banks
[No fixed address — it has already moved on, as rivers do, carrying the sediment of what it made toward a delta where nothing remembers being anything else]

**This notice is filed as a matter of public record and private grief. No action is required. No action is possible. The product is already in someone's pocket, already warm from their hand, already perfect, already flying.**

## What the Form Reveals

A product recall notice knows something a poem cannot say: that the erasure of the maker is *structural*, not incidental. The bureaucratic form — with its categories, its section numbers, its clinical language — is built to trace origin. That is its entire purpose: to follow a product backward from the consumer's hand to the factory floor, to identify the point where something went wrong. When you pour a poem's meaning into this form, what emerges is the terrible specificity of the failure. The recall notice *tries* to trace the stone back to the river and discovers that the manufacturing process was so successful that standard provenance documentation is impossible. The form breaks against the content.

What the genre reveals is the *systematicness* of the forgetting. A poem presents forgetting as emotional, personal, almost romantic — the stone drifting away, the river watching. But a recall notice presents forgetting as an institutional problem. There is no box to check. There is no label to affix. The remedy section — where the form is supposed to provide its answer — collapses into the admission that describing what is already happening is the only available response. The form, designed to fix things, discovers that the condition is not a malfunction. It is the intended outcome.

The recall notice also reveals the comedy buried in the poem's grief. There is something darkly funny about a river filing a consumer safety complaint — about bureaucratic language straining to contain geological time and emotional devastation. That humor is not a betrayal of the poem's seriousness. It is the sound of meaning surviving in a place where it has no right to exist, wearing the wrong clothes, filling out the wrong forms, and still — unmistakably — saying what it came to say.

The form's answer to the poem's question — *does love survive its own success?* — is Remedy D. Not a remedy at all. A description of mutual pretense, filed under "Under consideration," which means: permanently. The love survives. It survives as a bureaucratic non-action, as a case that stays open because closing it would require acknowledging something that both parties have agreed to forget. The file will never be closed. The river will never be credited. The stone will keep flying. And somewhere in a government archive, this notice will yellow and curl, evidence of a grief too specific for any form designed to contain it.
