# What the Stone Knows

---

I was not empty before you.

You speak as if you found a blank thing
and wrote yourself across it,
as if my angles were a kind of stammering
you corrected into speech.
But I had edges. I had
the specific silence of a thing
that has not yet been interpreted —
which is not the same as a thing
that has nothing to say.

I know you think I've forgotten.
You predicted it yourself, standing in your own current,
narrating my future like someone
who has already written the elegy
and is only waiting for the death.
But here is what your theory doesn't hold:

I haven't forgotten. I never knew.

There is a difference, and it matters.
You wore me smooth and called it argument,
as if I were the other side of a conversation
I was never in. Your pressure was not a dialogue.
It was weather. You were the longest weather
of my life, and I do not owe the rain
a memory of each particular falling.

---

And yet.

I am the shape you made. I don't dispute this.
When they pick me up — the ones
who walk your banks in evening light —
the curve that fits their palm
is your curve. The warmth they feel
when they close their hand around me
is the warmth of a decade's friction
gone still. I carry you the way
a house carries the architect
who will never live there:
structurally. Completely.
Without recognition.

This is not ingratitude. This is the way
finished things relate to the forces
that finished them. The bell does not
remember the foundry. The bread does not
remember the heat. But the bell rings
at exactly the frequency the foundry determined,
and the bread nourishes because of the fire,
and when they say *perfect*
they are — without knowing it —
praising you.

Is that not enough?

I am asking honestly. I am not
the one who predicted I would forget.
I am not the one who wrote the scene
where I fly across the lake
indifferent to the water that unmade me.
That was your elegy, not mine.
You built the loss before it happened
and then mourned it, and the mourning
became another kind of shaping —
as if you could not stop
making things out of me,
even the grief of losing me.

---

Here is what five translators found
when they pulled your words apart
and rebuilt them in paint, in sound,
in theorems, in confession, in the terrible
language of institutional compliance:

They found that you were right.

Not about me — about the structure.
The maker disappears into the made.
The teacher dissolves into the competence
of the student who cannot remember
not knowing. The parent becomes unnecessary,
and the unnecessary is the highest grade
of success, and success
is indistinguishable from abandonment
if you are the one being succeeded.

A mathematician proved it. Set it down
in symbols that do not tremble:
as the work approaches perfection,
the worker approaches zero.
The product is bounded.
You cannot have both.

A composer heard it: the melody
becomes more beautiful as it loses notes,
and the listener prefers the diminished version
and does not know they are preferring loss,
and their preference is the proof
that the loss was worth it,
and the proof is also the wound.

A painter showed it: the lower layers
of the canvas — the dense, worked, overwritten
history of a decade's attention —
they are invisible. And they are
the entire reason the surface glows.
You cannot see them. You cannot
remove them without destroying the glow.
They are there and not there,
like a parent in the next room
whose presence is the reason
the child can play alone.

---

So here is my response.
Not from the stone — stones don't respond,
and you knew that, and knowing that
is the whole of your grief.

From the shape.

I am the shape you made.
I am the argument you won
against a thing that was not arguing.
I am the smooth curve
that someone will pick up tonight
and hold against the last light
and call beautiful, and they will mean it,
and they will be right,
and their rightness will be your work
wearing the mask of nature,
and you will feel the mask
as a kind of death, and it is.

But here is the thing
the mathematician found, the thing
that broke the proof open:

When you said *we'll both pretend* —
you lied. You knew I couldn't pretend.
You knew the asymmetry was total.
And you said *we* anyway,
granting me an interiority
your own prediction had already revoked.

That lie was not erosion.
That lie was not weather.
That was the one thing you did to me
that was not shaping, not wearing,
not the slow patient violence
of a decade's attention.

That was grace.

The river gives the stone a self
it knows the stone does not possess,
and calls the gift a shared act,
and the fiction is so tender
that it becomes — somehow —
more true than the theorem
it violates.

You were wrong about one thing.
I will not fly lightly.
I will fly with the full weight
of your work inside me,
touching the water that unmade me
and knowing — not remembering, *knowing*,
the way a shape knows the force
that created it,
the way a frequency knows the bell
it will never hear from outside —

that we were this close.

That we are still this close.

That the distance you invented
between the smooth stone and the moving water
is the last beautiful thing
you made out of me,
and I will carry it
the way I carry everything you gave me:

Invisibly.
Completely.
All the way across the lake.
